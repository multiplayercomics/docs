Every Multic starts with a frame and a question: what happens next?

A **Multic** is an interactive comic. A creator combines art, dialogue,
narration, and story mechanics, then connects them into something a reader can
play. The result can move in a straight line, branch around a decision, or ask
several people to take part in the same story.

You make a Multic in **Studio**. Readers and players open it in Multic.

## What gives a Multic its shape

The current product vocabulary includes:

- **Frames** — the visual moments a reader sees.
- **Dialogue and Narration** — character speech and the voice around it.
- **Choices** — points where an answer can change the route through the story.
- **QTEs** — single, timer-bound prompts layered over a frame. A QTE can route
  through success, failure, or timeout.
- **Action Sequences** — video or image sequences with several timed cues and
  score-based exits. A cue can continue the sequence or route away from it.

Those pieces are connected rather than poured into a fixed page. That connected
structure is what lets the same Multic carry pacing, decisions, and different
routes through its story.

<Note>
  A QTE and an Action Sequence are separate mechanics. An Action Sequence may
  contain QTE-like timed cues, but it owns media, a cue timeline, and aggregate
  score or outcome routes.
</Note>

## Characters, locations, and a universe

A character can belong to a **universe** and be reused across the stories in
that universe. Studio keeps that character's identity and visual references as
a reusable record rather than asking you to rebuild the character in each
story.

Locations do not yet work as an equivalent first-class reusable library.
Creators can develop a location as a visual concept or reference and use a
location in story context and state, but the current product does not promise a
character-style location record shared across a universe.

## Solo and group play

A Multic can be read alone. Multics designed for group play can also give
several people authored slots in the same story. Slots can take different
routes, wait at a **Ready** point, resolve a **Shared choice** together or
separately, and compete or cooperate through a **Showdown**.

This is still a comic, but it borrows useful things from games: state, timing,
agency, and the possibility that another person changes the scene before you do.
