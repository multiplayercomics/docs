Every Multic starts with a frame and a question: what happens next?

A **Multic** is an interactive comic. You generate the art, write the dialogue,
and wire the choices — then publish it. Readers open it on their phone and play
through, tapping to make decisions, hitting timed moments, and branching the
story as they go. Some Multics they read alone. Some they play with friends, in
the same scene at the same time.

You make Multics in **Studio**. People play them in the **Player**.

## What goes into one

A Multic is built from a few kinds of pieces. You'll meet each in depth later —
here's the shape of it:

- **Frames** — the panels readers see, each one an image or a short video.
- **Dialogue and narration** — what your characters say, and the voice over the
  top.
- **Choices** — the branching points where the reader decides what happens next.
- **QTEs and action sequences** — timed moments that ask the player to act, not
  just watch.
- **Characters and locations** — your reusable cast and settings, consistent
  across every Multic in a universe.

## Solo or together

Most Multics are read alone. But a Multic can also be **played together**: two or
more people drop into the same story, each with their own role, making choices
that affect everyone in the scene. Group play is the part that makes a Multic feel
less like a comic and more like a game you're inside.
