Group play is part of the story's design. A creator declares who can take part,
where players wait for one another, and when their decisions converge or split.
Adding a second phone does not turn a solo story into a coherent multiplayer
one by itself.

<Note>
  This page describes the current authoring and runtime contract on the preview
  branch. It is not a launch announcement or a claim that group play has passed
  production device testing.
</Note>

## Start with player slots

A story can declare a stable set of **player slots**. Each slot has an ID and
can carry a label, colour, and role that Studio uses throughout the graph. With
no declared slots, the story uses one implicit solo slot named `self`.

A creator can configure a seat as:

- **Human** — a player takes that role.
- **AI easy, medium, or hard** — the runtime can operate the seat through the
  corresponding AI profile.
- **Observer-only** — the slot is present for watching rather than normal
  playable participation.

Slot IDs are the durable authoring primitive. Ready points, choices, routes,
and Showdowns refer to those IDs instead of relying on whoever happens to join
first.

## Bring the group together with Ready

A Frame can act as a barrier for every playable slot or for a named subset.
There are two ways to release it:

- **Arrival sync** — reaching the Frame counts as ready. The group continues
  when every required slot arrives.
- **Explicit Ready** — reaching the Frame is not enough. Each required player
  selects a creator-labelled button, which defaults to **Ready**.

The creator can add an auto-release timeout. Without one, the barrier waits for
every required slot. With one, the story can continue when the timer expires so
an absent player does not hold the group indefinitely.

## Resolve a Shared choice together or split

A **Shared choice** can wait for all required slots to submit a pick before it
resolves. The creator then chooses one of two shapes:

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Together" icon="merge">
    Resolve the submitted picks to one option, then send the group down the
    same route. The creator chooses how disagreement is settled, such as a
    majority, unanimity with a tiebreak, precedence, or seeded chance.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Split" icon="split">
    Route slots independently. Each player can follow their own pick, or the
    story can assign the available routes by a seeded random or weighted rule.
  </Card>
</Columns>

Shared choices can target all slots or a named subset. They can also have an
auto-resolve timeout, so a missing pick does not have to stall the story. The
timeout is authored for that choice; it is not a single global multiplayer
setting.

## Route a Showdown by outcome

A **Showdown** is a group-play mode on an **Action Sequence**. It collects a
score from each required slot, compares those scores using an authored outcome
model, and routes the result through named paths.

The current outcome models are:

- **Win / draw / lose** — the highest score wins; a tied highest score draws.
- **Ranked** — route each slot by placement, such as first or second.
- **Threshold** — compare scores with a minimum and route through pass or fail;
  the creator chooses how many players must clear the bar for the group to pass.

The creator connects each outcome to a route. A route can also be scoped to a
specific slot, which lets the same Showdown send different players to different
parts of the graph.

A Showdown can wait for every required score or resolve after its own timeout.
When an expected participant is absent, the realtime authority can provide a
deterministic AI takeover score. The automated contract refuses to replace a
present human player.

## Treat timeouts as story decisions

Ready barriers, Shared choices, and Showdowns each own their timeout. A timeout
keeps the session moving, but it also changes what “everyone participated”
means. Set it because the scene has a deliberate fallback, not because every
multiplayer mechanic should eventually give up.

AI seats and observer-only slots are authored at story level. A Showdown AI
takeover is different: it is a runtime fallback for a missing required
participant. Keeping those concepts separate makes replays and outcome routes
explainable.
